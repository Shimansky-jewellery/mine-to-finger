Chapter 01Northern Cape

Where are South African diamonds found?

Natural diamonds form deep within the earth and reach the surface through volcanic kimberlite pipes. South Africa's first diamonds were found in the Northern Cape in the 1860s, and Kimberley became the centre of the modern diamond world.

Over millions of years, rivers wore away some of those pipes and carried the diamonds downstream into ancient gravel beds along the [Vaal and Orange] rivers. Most Shimansky diamonds come from these alluvial deposits. The river journey tends to break weaker stones, which is why alluvial deposits are known for a high proportion of gem-quality diamonds.

Chapter 02Kimberley, since 1998

Where does a Shimansky diamond come from?

In 1998 our founder travelled to Kimberley and the river diggings to hand-select rough diamonds that meet the Shimansky standard. We still do. Most of our rough comes from alluvial diggings in the ancient riverbeds of the Northern Cape, and selecting a single diamond can take up to two days.

South Africa takes part in the Kimberley Process, the international certification for conflict-free rough diamonds. We go further: we select the rough ourselves, cut it in our own workshop, and keep one chain of custody all the way to Cape Town.

"In 1998 I bought my first rough diamonds in Kimberley. When they came back from the external cutter and polisher, they had been cut to keep every possible carat, not to release their light. I knew then that if I wanted the most beautiful diamonds, I would have to cut them myself."
Yair Shimansky, Founder

Chapter 03The Cape Town workshop

How do we decide how to cut a rough diamond?

Before a single facet is cut, every rough diamond is studied in our Cape Town workshop. We are looking for one answer: where is the light in this stone, and how do we release it?

  1. [Photo: rough in hand]

    Read the skin.

    Under a jeweller's loupe, we inspect the outer surface for anything that reaches from the surface into the stone.

  2. [Photo: loupe close-up]

    Open small windows.

    We polish a few tiny windows on the rough so we can see inside it.

  3. [Photo: polished window]

    Map the inside.

    Through those windows we find inclusions and anything that could remain in the polished shape we are planning.

  4. [Photo: rough beside its 3D scan]

    Scan in 3D.

    The rough is 3D-scanned to model the polished diamonds it could become.

  5. [Photo: marker line for the table]

    Place the table.

    We decide where the diamond's top face will sit, and whether to saw or polish from there. This decision sets the course of the entire cut.

Technology helps us see more. Experience decides what to do with what we see.

Chapter 04Why would a cutter saw a rough diamond in two?

One rough diamond. Two diamonds.

We first planned to polish this rough into one larger diamond. After mapping its inclusions and comparing cutting plans, we chose to saw it in two, aiming for better clarity and colour in each stone than the single larger diamond could offer. Sawing cannot be undone: days of study become one decision.

The finished diamonds and their GIA reports will show the result.
